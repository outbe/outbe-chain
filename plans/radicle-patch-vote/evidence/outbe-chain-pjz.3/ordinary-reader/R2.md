## Trust boundary assessment

`BlsEnvelope::context` reads bytes25..57, exactly where `RepositorySubject::new` stores context (`radicle-crypto/src/repository.rs:305–309,422`). This field remains covered by the signature. Ordinary readers accept its opaque application value, as explicitly authorized, but reconstruct every other subject field from the actual object.

For COB, `storage/git/cob.rs:75–98` parses the actual commit, obtains authorization, and calls `radicle_cob::load_native`; the latter reconstructs the subject from actual tree and exact unsigned commit metadata. For identity, the loaded action must identify the actual embedded blob, then verify under the actual entry author/repository (`identity/doc.rs:1080–1110`); evaluator root also requires founder=author (`cob/identity.rs:435–449`). For sigrefs, actual repository, canonical refs bytes and actual parent enter `verify_signed_refs` (`sigrefs/read.rs:84–119`); the prepared writer still fixes its own context. `BlsEnvelope::verify` (`repository.rs:458–477`) compares the entire reconstructed subject and required key, then verifies BLS over those exact bytes. Choosing the signed context therefore does not accept wrong objects, key substitution, metadata tampering or modified refs.

The ordinary reader proves native key/signature consistency, not current ValidatorSet/Vote eligibility. That is the explicit accepted scope. Managed policy still makes its separate local admission decision and cannot silently downgrade to the ordinary branch for the pinned owner.
