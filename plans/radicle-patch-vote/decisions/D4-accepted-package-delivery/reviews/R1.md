## Findings requiring proposal correction

### R1-F1 — Specify the actual total transfer budget and its accounting boundary

`proposal.md:85–91` names “existing native data-fetch limit” without identifying which existing limit or what it counts. They are different:

- `vendor/heartwood/crates/radicle-fetch/src/state.rs:25–30,98–114`: special refs 5 MiB, data refs 5 GiB, in `FetchLimit`.
- `vendor/heartwood/crates/radicle/src/node/config.rs:138,228–236`: configured wire pack receive limit, default 500 MiB, checked with `bytes >= limit`.

These are stream/pack limits; neither automatically limits the proposed sequence of individual raw-object/metadata requests, cumulative staging, retries, or repeated requests under a new request ID. `review::object_closure` (`integration/review.rs:490–532`) traverses an in-memory set and reads whole objects; it does not enforce either transfer budget. A frame cap and 256 pending requests alone therefore do not prove the claimed repository/archive bound.

Minimum correction: name the existing configuration/constant chosen and the exact accounting scope (one accepted receipt/transfer, raw decoded metadata plus distinct object bytes, staging plus retained partial data, checked cumulative counter before write/allocation). Explicitly state treatment of duplicates/retries, completion/EOF, and timeout cleanup/resume; a new request ID must not reset an unfinished transfer's total budget. Keep immutable/native generated-object caps separate from pinned code-object transfer, so “1 MiB metadata” does not silently become a new patch-content restriction. No new service or configurable limit is needed for this clarification.

### R1-F2 — Separate queues do not reserve the shared channel quota for signing

`proposal.md:85–92` simultaneously permits archive traffic on the existing 32 messages/s channel and promises not to block signatures/attestations. Existing `engine/stack/epoch/transport.rs:144–149` registers one quota for that channel. `integration/network.rs:787–888` owns one sender/receiver and command loop for endpoint and OSIG traffic. Separate archive queues/slots prevent inbox displacement, but do not prevent locally generated archive chunks from consuming that same channel's send quota before an OSIG message.

Minimum correction: state an explicit bounded archive scheduling/rate rule under the shared quota, reserving progress for OSIG/endpoint traffic, and a targeted mixed-traffic test. Queue separation alone must not be presented as the proof. Do not change consensus/DKG channels or introduce another service. A finite archive throughput/liveness assumption is acceptable; an unconditional “cannot block” claim is not established by the current wording.
